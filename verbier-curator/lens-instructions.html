Conduct an archive — how it works.

Music Lens turns Verbier Festival recordings into real-time re-orchestrations driven by your hands. Unlike traditional remix tools, it does not split the recording into instrument tracks — classical orchestral source separation from a stereo mixdown remains technically limited. Instead, the lens applies a small, carefully chosen chain of audio effects to the original mix and lets you reshape its character, space, and dynamics through gesture.

Why a "lens", not stems

Verbier recordings come as stereo mixdowns of live performances — main microphone pair plus room mics. In that form, individual orchestral voices are physically entangled: timpani and low strings overlap in time, frequency, and stereo image. Even state-of-the-art separators trained on pop music produce only rough, leaky stems on classical recordings.

The lens design takes the opposite stance: treat the mix as sacred, then sculpt it. With a focused chain of effects (3-band EQ, stereo width, reverb, compressor, harmonic/percussive balance, master gain), you can dramatically reshape the listening experience without ever pretending to "extract" what isn't recoverable from a stereo recording.

Gesture vocabulary

The conductor metaphor: your left hand shapes the sound, your right hand shapes the space, both hands together control the overall feel.

Right hand · space

Height ↕
Reverb wet — close ↔ cathedral
Side-to-side ↔

Make a fist
Strip the attacks (harmonic-only mix)

Both hands

  • Average height: master volume — raise both hands together for a crescendo, lower for diminuendo.
  • Distance between hands: compressor amount — pulled apart increases dynamic compression.

The mapping is symmetric: when you stop moving, the engine smoothly glides parameters back toward neutral so brief detection dropouts don't disrupt the sound.

Audio engine

The runtime engine is built on the Web Audio API, a graph of native nodes. There is no JavaScript DSP loop and no model inference at runtime.

The HPSS knob

Each piece is pre-processed with harmonic / percussive source separation (HPSS) — a classic algorithm that splits a recording into "sustained pitched content" and "transient attacks". At runtime, three audio sources play in sync: the original mix, the harmonic stem, and the percussive stem. The HPSS knob smoothly crossfades between them:

  • Center (0.5): only the original mix plays — pristine
  • Toward 1.0: fade out mix, fade in harmonic — smoother, less attack
  • Toward 0.0: fade out mix, fade in percussive — rhythmic skeleton

Troubleshooting

The sound seems unaffected by my hands

  • Check the parameter HUD in the top-right of the demo — the values should change as you move.
  • If you've been adjusting sliders, gesture control pauses for 2 seconds after the last slider movement.
  • Try the Sliders button in the toolbar to see if effects work without gestures.

Camera was denied or unavailable

The app falls back to slider-only mode automatically. All eight effects are accessible via on-screen sliders, and presets work the same.

Audio sounds glitchy or has clicks

  • The engine smooths every parameter change with a 20ms ramp, but extreme reverb / compressor settings combined with low system performance can introduce artifacts.
  • Try the Original preset to confirm clean playback baseline.
  • Closing other tabs and using a dedicated audio output (not Bluetooth) helps.

Playback drifts away from the video

The demo uses audio as the master clock and re-syncs the video every 250ms. A drift larger than 0.4s triggers a hard re-seek; smaller drifts are corrected by tiny playback-rate adjustments.

Privacy: the camera feed is processed entirely in your browser via MediaPipe Hands. No video frames are ever sent over the network.
